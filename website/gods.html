Gods of the Eternal City

The gods are not distant myths here - they are active presences in the world, watching, favoring, and occasionally interfering directly in mortal affairs. Some are stewarded by staff or trusted god-players; all shape the fate of Rome in ways both subtle and unmistakable.

The Twelve Olympians

Jupiter

King of the Gods · Sky, Law, and Thunder

Jupiter rules from a throne of storm and stone atop Olympus, arbiter of oaths and final word in matters no mortal court can settle. He favors those who claim authority boldly and rules those beneath him - gods and mortals alike - with equal parts justice and thunder. It is said his eyes miss nothing that happens beneath his sky.

Juno

Queen of the Gods · Marriage, Loyalty, and Vengeance

Juno presides over oaths of marriage and household, and woe to any who break them carelessly in her sight. Fiercely loyal to those who honor their bonds, she is an equally patient and thorough avenger of those who do not. Politicians and schemers court her favor at their peril - she remembers everything.

Neptune

God of the Sea, Earthquakes, and Horses

The seas answer to Neptune's temper as surely as the tides answer to the moon, and the ground itself has been known to shake at his displeasure. Sailors, traders, and cavalry alike keep small shrines to him, hoping to be remembered kindly rather than swept aside.

Ceres

Goddess of the Harvest, Growth, and the Seasons

Ceres's grief and joy quite literally shape the turning of the year - the world is said to wither when her daughter is lost to the Underworld, and bloom again at her return. Farmers, nymphs, and anyone who has ever gone hungry keep her close in their prayers.

Minerva

Goddess of Wisdom, Strategy, and Craft

Born fully-armored from the mind of Jupiter himself, Minerva favors the clever over the merely strong - a well-laid plan over a brute charge, a fortified line over a wild rush. Legionaries who study formation as much as they train the blade often find her favor easiest to earn.

Apollo

God of Prophecy, Light, Music, and Healing

Apollo's gift of foresight comes at a price - his oracles speak truth, but rarely plainly. Those who read the flight of birds or the shape of entrails in his name walk a fine line between insight and madness, seeing futures they cannot always prevent.

Diana

Goddess of the Hunt, the Wild, and the Moon

Diana roams the untamed edges of the world, fiercely protective of wild things and utterly without mercy for those who abuse them. Hunters and trackers who take only what they need, and no more, are the ones who find her favor lasting.

Mars

God of War, Bloodlust, and Battle-Fury

Where Minerva is the general's cold calculation, Mars is the soldier's hot blood - the roar of a charge, the thrill of a blade finding its mark. He cares little for the reasons behind a war, only for the fury and glory found within it. The arena sands are said to be among his favorite places on earth.

Venus

Goddess of Love, Beauty, and Desire

Venus's influence moves through courts and bedchambers alike - a well-placed glance, a whispered promise, an alliance sealed with more than words. Senators who underestimate her power in politics rarely stay in power long.

Vulcan

God of the Forge, Fire, and Craftsmanship

Cast out from Olympus for his lameness and welcomed back only for the unmatched weapons and wonders he could build, Vulcan values skill above beauty and function above pride. The Cyclopes are said to be his oldest and most loyal forge-hands, and any smith worth their hammer keeps a shrine to him near the coals.

Mercury

God of Travelers, Trade, Thieves, and Messages

Quick-tongued and quicker-footed, Mercury moves between the world of gods and mortals more freely than any other Olympian - and he's just as comfortable delivering a message as he is picking a pocket along the way. Scouts, spies, and merchants all claim him as patron, usually for different reasons.

Vesta

Goddess of the Hearth, Home, and the Sacred Flame

Vesta is the quietest of the Twelve and, in some ways, the most load-bearing - her sacred fire has burned unbroken in the Forum for as long as Rome has stood, tended day and night by the Vestal Virgins. Every household hearth is a small echo of hers, and every family that keeps it lit is, knowingly or not, keeping faith with her.

Other Powers

Not every power that shapes Rome sits among the Twelve. These three are older, stranger, and no less real for being feared - or indulged - rather than openly worshipped in the Forum's temples.

Pluto

Lord of the Underworld, Master of the Dead

Pluto rules the realm beneath the world with a grim, orderly fairness rarely credited to him above ground - he steals no one who isn't already owed to him. Every soul that falls in the arena, on the battlefield, or simply in the fullness of time eventually stands before his gates. It is said a determined few have talked, fought, or tricked their way back out.

Trivia

Goddess of Crossroads, Magic, and the Night

Trivia stands at every threshold - between roads, between worlds, between choices not yet made. She grants power to those willing to walk unlit paths, and her secret cult meets where three roads cross to trade in favors most gods above would refuse to grant.

Bacchus

God of Wine, Revelry, and Ecstatic Release

Bacchus arrived in Rome late and strange, a god of intoxication, theater, and the wild loss of self that comes with both - never quite one of the Twelve, and worshipped all the more fervently for it. The wine flows generously in his name at every feast and every arena celebration, though the Senate itself once moved to restrict his rites when they grew too unruly for the city's comfort - and those who drink too deep still sometimes find his gifts come with a price.

Devotion & Piety

You can do more than admire the gods from a distance - devote yourself to one directly, and real piety brings real, tangible favor.

Choosing a Patron

Pray to any of the fourteen gods above to begin following them. It's a real, permanent commitment - true devotion, not a passing gesture - and entirely separate from faction membership, so a Legionary devoted to Mars is one of the most natural combinations in Rome.

Earning Piety

Piety grows through action in that god's own domain, not just by praying. Mars rewards a proven blade in battle, Mercury a good trade, Apollo a life saved through healing, and Pluto surviving death itself and returning from it.

Real, Tangible Favor

Rise to Devoted, then Beloved, and the blessing becomes mechanical, not just narrative: Mars sharpens your blade, Mercury sweetens every deal, Apollo's touch heals deeper, and Pluto's faithful face a lighter hand from death itself.
